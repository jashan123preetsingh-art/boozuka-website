How coaching institutes in Punjab can get more admissions online

Most coaching and IELTS institutes in Punjab do not have a lead problem. They have a follow-up problem. Students enquire on Instagram, WhatsApp, Google and through walk-ins, but many of those enquiries are never answered quickly, never tracked and never followed up. This guide shows how to fix that, step by step, before you spend another rupee on ads.

Why admissions are lost online

Think about how a student actually chooses an institute today. They search "IELTS coaching near me", look at two or three Google Maps listings, open your Instagram, maybe your website, and then send a WhatsApp message to more than one institute. The first institute that replies clearly, with batch timings and a simple next step, usually gets the demo class.

That means admissions are lost in small gaps, not in one big failure:

  • Slow replies. A message sent at 9 pm is seen the next afternoon. By then the student has booked a demo somewhere else.
  • No tracking. Enquiries sit in a counsellor's personal phone. Nobody knows how many came in this week, from where, or what happened to them.
  • No second follow-up. Students say "I will think about it". Without a reminder, nobody calls back.
  • Weak first impression. An old Google listing, no fee or batch information, or a website that does not open well on a phone.

The good news: each of these gaps can be closed with simple tools your team already knows, like WhatsApp Business and Google Sheets.

Step 1: Get found where students search

Google Business Profile

For local searches, your Google Business Profile often matters more than your website. Make sure it has the right primary category (for example "Coaching center" or "Language school"), accurate timings, your phone and WhatsApp number, and real photos of your classrooms, reception and faculty at work. Add each course as a service with a short description. Post an update when a new batch starts. We explain this in more detail in our Google Maps ranking guide.

A website built for admissions

Your website does not need twenty pages. It needs to answer the questions every student asks, on one screen of a phone: which courses you run, batch timings, whether there is a free demo class, where you are, and one clear button to enquire on WhatsApp. If fees are not public, say how to get them ("shared on WhatsApp after your demo"). Students trust institutes that are clear.

Instagram that answers questions

Reels about tips, mock test practice and student-life moments build trust. Keep the bio simple: courses, location and a WhatsApp link. Pin a post with batch timings so new visitors do not have to ask.

Step 2: Reply to every enquiry within minutes

Speed is the easiest advantage you can build. Students compare institutes in real time, and the one that replies first with useful information usually wins the conversation.

  • Set up WhatsApp Business properly. Add a greeting message, an away message with your timings, quick replies for fees, batches and location, and labels such as "New", "Demo booked" and "Admitted".
  • Use an auto-reply for after-hours messages. A short message such as "Thanks for your message. IELTS evening batch runs 6 to 8 pm. Reply 1 for fee details, 2 to book a free demo" keeps the student engaged until a counsellor calls.
  • Decide who owns replies. One person or a small rota. If everyone is responsible, nobody is.

If you get a lot of messages, a WhatsApp chatbot can handle the routine questions and pass serious students to your counsellor. Our WhatsApp automation guide explains what it can and cannot do.

Step 3: Track every lead in one sheet

You cannot improve what you cannot see. A simple admissions CRM in Google Sheets is enough for most institutes. Each enquiry gets one row with:

  • Name and WhatsApp number
  • Course of interest
  • Source: Meta ad, Google Maps, website, walk-in, referral
  • Status: New, Contacted, Demo booked, Follow-up, Admitted, Not interested
  • Next step and date

Review the sheet every morning for ten minutes. Who needs a call today? Which demos are booked? Which source brought the students who actually joined? After a month, you will know which channels deserve more budget and which do not.

Step 4: Follow up like a system, not a favour

Most students need more than one touchpoint before they decide. Write down a simple follow-up sequence and stick to it:

  1. Same day: reply with course details and an invitation to a free demo class.
  2. Day 2: a short call or voice note from a counsellor to answer questions.
  3. Day 4: share something useful, such as a band score tip or a sample mock test.
  4. Before the batch starts: a reminder that seats are filling and the start date.

Be polite and stop if the student says no. The goal is to be helpful and present, not pushy. Reminders can be automated so they happen even on busy days.

Step 5: Run ads only after the basics work

Meta and Google ads can bring a steady flow of enquiries, but ads multiply whatever system you already have. If replies are slow and follow-up is missing, ads simply bring more enquiries that you lose. Fix steps 1 to 4 first, then start with a small, clearly targeted campaign: one course, one location radius, one offer such as a free demo class, and a WhatsApp button that drops leads straight into your sheet.

Track cost per enquiry and, more importantly, enquiries that turned into admissions. That is the number that tells you whether ads are working.

A simple weekly checklist

  • Google Business Profile: one new photo or post
  • WhatsApp: every message from the week answered and labelled
  • Sheet: every lead has a status and a next step
  • Follow-ups: all due calls made
  • Reviews: ask happy students who finished a course for an honest Google review

Speak to parents, not only students

For school-level coaching, and often for IELTS too, parents are part of the decision. They look for different things than students: safety, discipline, faculty experience, regular progress updates and clear communication. Make sure your website and WhatsApp replies answer their questions as well.

  • Explain how you share progress, for example a monthly report or a short parent meeting.
  • Show your classrooms and reception in real photos, so parents know what to expect.
  • Offer a call with a counsellor, not only a chat, for families who prefer to talk.

Small touches like these often decide between two institutes with similar courses.

What to measure each month

You do not need complicated dashboards. Four numbers, taken from your sheet, tell you most of what you need to know:

  • Enquiries by source
  • Demo classes booked
  • Admissions, and which source they came from
  • Average reply time during working hours

If enquiries are high but demos are low, work on your replies and information. If demos are high but admissions are low, look at the demo class experience and your follow-up. This keeps decisions simple and based on your own data.

Questions institute owners ask

Do I need a website if I already have Instagram?

Instagram is great for trust, but a simple website gives Google something to rank, answers common questions in one place and looks more established to parents. It does not need to be big. One focused, mobile-first page can be enough to start.

Should I show fees publicly?

It depends on your positioning. Many institutes share fees on WhatsApp after a demo class. Whatever you choose, tell students clearly how to get fee details so they do not have to guess.

How quickly should we reply to enquiries?

As quickly as you realistically can, ideally within minutes during working hours. An automatic reply after hours keeps the conversation alive until a counsellor calls.

Is Google Sheets enough as a CRM?

For most small and mid-sized institutes, yes. It is free, your team already knows it and it can be shared. You can move to a custom CRM later if your volume grows.
